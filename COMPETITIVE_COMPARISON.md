# Comparison with the Cinematic Strawberry construction

The comparison is between two different closed-tree implementations. The reference for the earlier work is Alexander Filin's 14 September 2026 manuscript and its accompanying Lean development at `47edfaa400209dee85837e032dec0620915004e5`. The comparison concerns the mathematical constructions and their implementations.

## Numerical output

The certified endpoint returns an arbitrary unary natural number for every finite deterministic tape table and every unary input. Both its bare macro and its eight-mode primitive policy use the original binary scheduler. A regular guard decides when to read. The numeric reader folds over literal physical slices and uses Cinematic Strawberry's seed-derived full-row parser; the latter is a directly credited upstream strength. The first-hit cutoff now allows an arbitrary total numerical reader of the concrete replacement registers. A proof relating this particular whole-register class to every comparison class used in Filin's paper has not been established.

The upstream paper's primitive public source-output field in its headline is a scanned bit, alongside full physical tape rows from which arbitrary unary outputs can also be read. We claim no separation of computable functions between the two results. V2 proves the particular numerical interface and primitive-boundary correspondence for this online simulator. Neither an end-to-end runtime advantage on universal tape inputs nor an authorship or submission-priority advantage follows. The earlier paper's window and decoder cost results remain separate.
